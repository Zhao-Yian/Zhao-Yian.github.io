Overview

Long-horizon stability without a teacher, critic, or long videos.

Streaming video world models generate the future chunk by chunk, conditioned on their own past and a stream of actions. When trained on short clips, they drift once a rollout exceeds the training horizon as prediction errors re-enter the context and compound — causing scenes on DROID to dissolve into saturated lattice textures and camera rollouts on Sekai to darken and fade to black.

A common remedy is distribution matching distillation (DMD) on self-generated rollouts. For action-conditioned world models, however, the teacher becomes a bottleneck: an external video model is agnostic to the actions, whereas the model's own pretrained network drifts in the same manner as the student. Teacher-free per-sample objectives avoid a teacher, yet supervise only states paired with recorded ground truth within short clips.

We observe that a long rollout need not match a teacher, provided it remains a realistic continuation of its own scene that follows the commanded actions. Fréchet Forcing fine-tunes a world model together with its deployed multi-step sampler under three complementary constraints derived solely from short real clips: generative anchoring, context fidelity, and reality matching.

Fine-tuning MiniWorld-1B (pretrained on 253-frame clips) for at most 36 GPU-hours on 381-frame clips yields MiniWorld-1B-FF, whose rollouts remain stable beyond 4,000 frames (>10× the fine-tuning horizon) and accurately follow robot and camera commands.

Method

Fréchet Forcing

A long rollout remains effective when it acts as a faithful conditional generator, preserves the identity of its initial scene, and produces realistic frames. Fréchet Forcing enforces these three properties with unit-weighted constraints.

ℒtotal = ℒanc Generative anchoring + ℒctx Context fidelity + ℒreal Reality matching

All distributional terms use running moment estimates (EMA decay β = 0.99) and self-normalised Fréchet distances FD / sg(FD), enabling batch-size-one training per GPU without storing past features.

01 · Regression on short window

Generative Anchoring (ℒanc)

Applies the flow-matching (FM) loss on W = 4 real chunks conditioned on a self-generated history of c ∼ U[1, N] chunks re-noised to ta = 0.7 and denoised with the deployed staircase sampler.

hc = [ sg S(G, x<c-1ta, a), xc-1 ]

Ending on real data eliminates seam flicker. Replacing the final history chunk with the clean real chunk xc-1 resolves the spatial mismatch across the boundary, preventing the model from periodically redrawing the scene every chunk.

02 · Clip-centred latent FD

Context Fidelity (ℒctx)

During a free rollout of N = 40 chunks from a single real latent, gradients flow through the final K = 8 chunks. Each latent is summarised by per-channel spatial mean and std concatenated across consecutive latents ψ(z), then centred on the clip's own ground truth:

h = ψ(zN-K:N) − mean(ψ(xN-K:N))

Preserving scene identity. Centring converts a marginal population metric into a clip-conditional one — preventing rollouts from collapsing toward a dataset-average appearance while scoring only the gradient-carrying tail.

03 · Perceptual frame FD

Reality Matching (ℒreal)

Decodes the last D = 4 rollout chunks (plus one preceding warm-up chunk whose frames are discarded) through the frozen causal VAE decoder and embeds frames with the CLS token of a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14.

ℒreal = FDEMA( φ(Dec(z)), refreal )

Ruling out degenerate textures. Matching high-level perceptual moments against VAE reconstructions of real training frames penalises the dark, washed-out, or fish-scale tiled artifacts that satisfy low-order latent moments.

Algorithm 1 · One training step of Fréchet Forcing PyTorch-like pseudocode 
# G: streaming world model;  S: deployed streaming sampler
# Dec: frozen VAE decoder;  phi: frozen DINOv2;  psi: latent moments
x, a = sample_clip()                    # 381-frame real latents & actions

# 1. Generative anchoring: FM after a self-denoised history
c = randint(1, N)
hist = S(G, renoise(x[:c], t=t_a), a).detach()
hist[-1] = x[c-1]                       # history ends on clean real chunk
L_anc = flow_matching(G, x[c:c+W], hist, a)

# 2. Free rollout from one real latent; last K chunks carry gradients
z = S(G, x[0], a)[-K:]
x_z = x[N-K:N]                          # GT over the same temporal span

# 3. Context fidelity & Reality matching via self-normalised EMA FD
L_ctx  = FD_ema(psi(z) - psi(x_z).mean(0), ref_ctx, ema_ctx)
L_real = FD_ema(phi(Dec(z)), ref_real, ema_real)

(L_anc + L_ctx + L_real).backward()
optimizer.step()

Training dynamics

Why naive distribution matching fails

Diagnostic probes over training steps reveal three distinct distributional failure modes on free rollouts — and show how each design choice in Fréchet Forcing prevents them.

1. Context decoupling. Matching an unconditional marginal FD allows rollouts to ignore their initial frame and collapse toward a dataset-average reddish wash (slope drops from 0.92 to 0.50 in 50 steps). Clip centring fixes this.

2. Gradient–statistic misalignment. Pooling statistics over all N chunks while truncating gradients to the last K forces tail chunks to overshoot to compensate for the detached prefix (alignment cosine decays to 0.39). Scoring only the graded tail resolves it.

3. Feature-space exploitation. Optimising low-order latent moments alone drives training FD down while held-out CLIP-FD rises, producing dark or fish-scale tiled frames. Adding DINOv2 reality matching closes this loophole.

4. Chunk-boundary seam redraw. Re-rendering the anchoring history all the way to the seam teaches the model to redraw the scene every 8 frames (flicker 1.31). Ending the history on one clean real chunk lowers flicker to 1.07 (matching GT).
